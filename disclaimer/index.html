Medical disclaimer

What Warmlit is for, what it doesn’t do, and when to talk to your doctor. Warmlit shows the same information in the app.

Effective October 28, 2026Part of our Terms of Use

Important health information

Warmlit is a personal logbook and reminder tool. It is not a regulated medical device. It has not been cleared or approved by the FDA and is not CE- or UKCA-marked. It does not diagnose or treat and does not give medical advice.

Warmlit never calculates, recommends or changes a dose. Always take your medicine exactly as prescribed and follow the Medication Guide or patient leaflet that came with it. Check with your doctor or pharmacist before you start, stop, skip, delay or change a dose, and before making any other decision about your treatment.

Waiting-period timers use the general instructions printed in product labeling and the settings you choose. Your prescriber’s instructions come first. Reminders depend on your iPhone’s settings and can be delayed or missed, so don’t rely on Warmlit as your only reminder.

Charts, summaries and reports show only what you entered or allowed Warmlit to read from Apple Health. They may be incomplete or wrong and are not a substitute for professional care.

If you think you may be having a medical emergency, call 911 (US), 112 (EU) or your local emergency number now. For questions about side effects, contact your healthcare professional. In the US you can also report side effects to the FDA at fda.gov/medwatch. In the EU, report them to your national medicines agency.

What Warmlit is for

Warmlit is a personal record-keeping and reminder app for adults who have been prescribed a GLP-1 medicine. It lets you record the doses you take, set reminders for the schedule your prescriber gave you, time the waiting period printed in your medicine’s instructions, note injection sites and side effects, see your own weight, protein and water entries, and export a report to share with your healthcare professional. Warmlit does not diagnose, monitor or treat any disease or condition, does not calculate, recommend or change doses, and does not interpret symptoms or measurements.

Waiting-period timers and label information

Some medicines come with instructions about how to take them, such as taking a tablet on an empty stomach and then waiting before you eat, drink or take other medicines. Warmlit’s timers use the general instructions printed in product labeling, and Warmlit shows where each instruction comes from.

Labeling differs between countries and products, and it changes over time. If your prescriber or pharmacist told you something different, follow them.

Charts, reports and recaps

  • Doctor reports contain patient-recorded data from Warmlit. The data hasn’t been verified by a clinician and isn’t medical advice.
  • Weekly recaps are written on your iPhone by on-device AI from your own entries or, on iPhones without Apple Intelligence on iOS 26 or later, from a simple template. They can contain mistakes and aren’t medical advice.

When to contact your doctor

Warmlit includes a calm “When to contact your doctor” screen based on the warnings in product labeling. It’s a reminder, not a complete list. Read the Medication Guide or leaflet that came with your medicine, and contact your healthcare professional if you’re worried about any symptom.

Medicine names and trademarks

Medicine brand names are trademarks of their owners. Warmlit shows them only in its medication picker, in plain text, so you can identify the medicine you were prescribed. Questions

We can’t answer medical questions; please ask your doctor or pharmacist. For questions about Warmlit itself, email admin@ccoders.eu or visit Support.
