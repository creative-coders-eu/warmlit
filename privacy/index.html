In short

  • Warmlit has no account. Apart from the optional meal photo estimates below, the app doesn’t send personal data to us, and we can’t see what you log.
  • Your entries are stored on your iPhone, and Warmlit never sends them to us or to anyone else. They’re included in your own iPhone backups, and anything you choose to save to Apple Health is managed by Apple.
  • No analytics, ads, tracking or third-party SDKs in the app. No cookies, analytics or trackers on this website.
  • Apple processes all payments. We never see your payment details.
  • If you email us, we use your message only to help you. Please don’t send health details.
  • You have rights under the GDPR, including the right to complain to the Polish data protection authority (UODO).

2. What this policy covers

This policy covers:

  • this website, creative-coders-eu.github.io/warmlit; and

Apple’s own services, such as the App Store, your Apple Account, iCloud, Apple Health, Apple Intelligence and TestFlight, are covered by Apple’s Privacy Policy.

3. What the app sends, and what it doesn’t

Meal photo estimates (Warmlit Pro, optional): when you choose to analyse a photo of your food, the photo and proof of your Pro purchase go to our server, which sends the photo to Anthropic to estimate calories, protein, carbs and fiber. We don’t store the photo or the estimate; Anthropic may keep the request for up to 30 days for safety monitoring and doesn’t use it to train its models. Details are in section 3a.

  • Warmlit has no account and no sign-in. Apart from meal photo estimates, it doesn’t send what you log, how you use the app, or any identifier to us or to anyone else.
  • It contains no third-party code that collects data: no analytics, advertising, attribution or crash-reporting SDKs.
  • It doesn’t track you across apps or websites, and it never asks for permission to do so.
  • Warmlit’s App Store privacy label lists only what meal photo estimates send.
  • We can’t see or access what you log unless you send it to us, and we can’t recover it for you. That also means we can’t restore it if you lose your iPhone without a backup, so keep your own copy (see Backups and new devices).

Because your entries are processed only on your iPhone, at your request and under your control, we don’t process them as a controller or as a processor.

3a. Meal photo estimates (Warmlit Pro, optional)

  • What is sent, and when. Only when you take or pick a photo and tap to analyse it: the photo, reduced in size, and the signed proof of your Warmlit Pro purchase that Apple puts on your iPhone. Nothing else you log is sent.
  • Who receives it. Our server, which runs on Cloudflare and acts as our processor, checks your purchase and the limits, then sends the photo to Anthropic (Claude), also our processor, which returns an estimate. You review and edit the estimate before anything is saved on your iPhone.
  • How long it’s kept. We don’t store the photo or the estimate. To apply the photo limits, our server keeps a counter linked to your purchase’s transaction number for up to 62 days. Anthropic may keep the request for up to 30 days for safety monitoring and doesn’t use it to train its models.
  • Legal basis. Your explicit consent, which Warmlit asks for before the first photo (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a) and 9(2)(a); for US residents, the consent described in our Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy). You can withdraw it at any time by turning meal photos off in Warmlit’s settings; that stops all further sending.
  • Transfers. Cloudflare and Anthropic may process the request in the United States, under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework or the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses.

4. What Warmlit stores on your iPhone

Warmlit keeps what you enter, and what it needs to work, on your iPhone:

  • your medications and schedules: the medicine, its form, the dose and dose history as prescribed, reminder times and, where it applies, the waiting period;
  • the doses you log: date and time, dose, whether it was taken, skipped or missed, injection site, waiting-period times and notes;
  • side effects: the symptom, how severe it was, and notes;
  • weight, protein and water entries;
  • your settings, such as units, goals and reminder preferences;
  • a small summary that Warmlit’s widgets and Live Activity display.

This data is kept in Warmlit’s private storage on your iPhone, in a container that only Warmlit and its own widgets can open, and it’s protected by the encryption built into iOS. You can also turn on Face ID lock in Warmlit’s settings. To guard against data loss, Warmlit can also keep a few automatic copies of your entries in its private storage. Like your entries, they’re stored on your iPhone and included in your iPhone backups.

It stays until you delete it. You can edit or delete entries at any time, erase everything Warmlit stores on your iPhone, including the automatic copies, with Delete all data in Warmlit’s settings, or delete the app. Copies outside Warmlit aren’t affected: files you exported stay where you saved them, older iPhone backups may still contain Warmlit data until they’re replaced, and entries Warmlit saved to Apple Health stay there unless you choose to delete them too.

Where a US state’s law requires it, Warmlit asks Apple for the age range of your Apple Account (for example, “18 or older”) when it opens, to make sure you’re an adult. Apple decides when this applies and shows its own screens. Warmlit uses the answer only on your iPhone, at that moment, and doesn’t store it or send it to us or anyone else.

5. Apple Health

Apple Health is optional and free, and every feature also works with entries you make by hand. When you turn on Sync with Apple Health in Warmlit’s settings, iOS shows Apple’s permission screen, where you choose exactly what Warmlit may read and write.

  • Read: weight, dietary protein and water, so Warmlit can show them next to your dose history.
  • Write: the weight, protein and water entries you log in Warmlit.
  • Nothing else: Warmlit doesn’t read medications from Apple Health and doesn’t save side effects or symptoms to it.
  • Warmlit reads information from Apple Health only while it shows it to you or creates a doctor report you asked for, and doesn’t keep a copy in its own storage.
  • Warmlit never uses information from Apple Health for advertising, marketing or data mining, never sells it or shares it with anyone, and never stores it in iCloud. It leaves Warmlit only if you create a doctor report that includes it and choose where to send the report (see Exports and files you share).
  • To change or withdraw access, open the Health app, tap your profile picture, then Apps, then Warmlit. You can also turn sync off in Warmlit’s settings.
  • Entries that Warmlit saved to Apple Health stay there until you delete them. You can delete them in the Health app, or choose to delete them too when you use Delete all data in Warmlit.

6. Weekly recap and on-device AI

With Warmlit Pro, Warmlit can write a short recap of your week. On iPhones with iOS 26 or later that support Apple Intelligence, the recap is written by Apple’s on-device language model, on your iPhone. Warmlit doesn’t use Apple’s Private Cloud Compute or any third-party AI service, and nothing you log is sent anywhere to write it. On other iPhones, or when Apple Intelligence is off, Warmlit uses a simple template instead. A recap only restates what you logged.

A recap is created when you open it and isn’t saved. To use the template instead of AI, turn off “Write with on-device AI” on the recap screen.

7. Reminders, Live Activities and widgets

Reminders are local notifications scheduled on your iPhone. Warmlit doesn’t use push notifications or a server to send them. The Live Activity, widgets and controls read a small summary from Warmlit’s storage on your iPhone.

They’re discreet by default: reminders, the Live Activity and widgets don’t show your medicine’s name or dose unless you turn on “Show medicine names” in Warmlit’s settings. You can also hide notification previews in the iPhone Settings app under Notifications.

Notifications, Live Activities, alarms and Face ID are optional. You can turn them on or off at any time in the iPhone Settings app under Apps, then Warmlit. Warmlit works without them, but without notifications it can’t remind you.

8. Exports and files you share

When you export a CSV file, a backup file or (with Warmlit Pro) the doctor report, Warmlit creates the file on your iPhone and opens the iOS share sheet. You decide where it goes, for example to Files, AirDrop, email or your doctor, and whoever receives it handles it under their own terms. We don’t get a copy. With Apple Health sync on, the doctor report can also include weight, protein and water from Apple Health. Files you import are read on your iPhone and aren’t sent anywhere.

9. Backups and new devices

Warmlit doesn’t use iCloud Drive, CloudKit or any other iCloud storage or sync for your entries. Like other apps’ data, Warmlit’s data is included in your iPhone backups (iCloud Backup or a backup on your computer) and moves to a new iPhone when you restore a backup or transfer directly. Those backups are controlled by you and Apple, under Apple’s Privacy Policy. Warmlit doesn’t sync between devices.

10. Purchases

Apple sells Warmlit Pro. Apple handles the personal data involved in purchases, payments, refunds and subscription management as an independent controller, under Apple’s Privacy Policy and the Apple Media Services Terms and Conditions. In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, that’s Apple Distribution International Ltd. in Ireland. We never receive your name, email address, Apple Account details or payment details. Warmlit checks what you’ve bought on your iPhone using Apple’s StoreKit.

Apple gives us sales and subscription reports. They can include your country and a subscriber number that Apple creates just for us, but not your name or contact details, so we can’t tell who you are. We use these reports only for accounting and tax, which the law requires (GDPR Art. 6(1)(c)), and to understand how Warmlit Pro is doing, which is our legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)). We keep them as long as accounting and tax law requires.

11. Crash reports from Apple

If you’ve chosen to share analytics with app developers (in the iPhone Settings app: Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements → Share With App Developers), Apple may give us crash reports and aggregated statistics, such as how often the app is used or crashes. They don’t contain what you log in Warmlit, and they don’t identify you to us.

We use them to find and fix problems and, in aggregate, to understand how Warmlit is used, for example how many people keep using it. That’s based on our legitimate interest in keeping Warmlit reliable and improving it (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)). We delete crash reports we download within 12 months. You can stop sharing at any time in the same setting.

12. Beta testing with TestFlight

If you test Warmlit through Apple’s TestFlight, Apple runs the beta under its own privacy policy. If we invite you by email, we enter your name and email address in App Store Connect so that Apple can send you the invitation. If you join through a public link, Apple doesn’t show us your name or email address. TestFlight automatically collects crash reports and usage information, such as your device model, iOS version, installs and sessions, and Apple makes them available to us together with any feedback, screenshots and comments you send. Apple keeps feedback for a year, and may keep crash reports and usage information until the problems are fixed.

If we invite you because you take a GLP-1 medicine, we also keep a short tester list with your name, your email address and whether you use a pill or a shot or are switching from another app. We use all of this only to run the beta and fix problems (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b) and (f)). Apart from the email invitation described above, we don’t share it, and we delete our copies within 12 months after the beta ends.

Feedback, screenshots, the tester list and an email invitation can reveal health information, such as that you take a GLP-1 medicine, a dose or a symptom. We use that health information only to run the beta, based on your explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a)), which we ask for before we add you to the tester list; it also covers our email invitation and health details in your feedback. If you haven’t given it, for example because you joined through a public link, we don’t use health details in your feedback, and we delete feedback that contains them once we’ve noted the problem it reports. You can withdraw your consent at any time by asking us to delete that information; that doesn’t affect what we did before. Before you send a screenshot, check that it doesn’t show health details you’d rather keep private.

13. When you email us

  • What: your email address, your name if you include it, your message and any attachments.
  • Why: to answer you and to handle your request or complaint.
  • Legal basis: taking steps under our agreement with you (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)), our legitimate interest in answering people who contact us (Art. 6(1)(f)), and our legal duty to handle complaints (Art. 6(1)(c)).
  • Please don’t send health details. We can help without knowing your medicines, doses or symptoms, so please don’t attach exports or screenshots of your log either. If you include health information anyway, we’ll ask whether we may use it to answer you, and use it only if you agree; that’s your explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a)). If you don’t agree, we’ll help without it and delete your message once we’ve answered. You can withdraw your consent at any time by asking us to delete your messages.
  • Who handles it: our email provider, Zoho Corporation B.V. (the Netherlands), which hosts our mailbox in its EU data centres and acts as our processor.
  • How long: we delete support emails 24 months after your last message, unless we need them longer to establish, exercise or defend a legal claim.

14. This website

  • This website uses no cookies, analytics, tracking pixels, external fonts or third-party scripts. It’s plain HTML and CSS.
  • It’s hosted on GitHub Pages by GitHub, Inc. in the United States. When you visit, your browser sends GitHub your IP address and standard request details, and GitHub logs visitors’ IP addresses for security purposes. We don’t receive these logs. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in running a secure website (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)). See GitHub’s Privacy Statement.
  • Our pages ask your browser not to tell other sites that you came from here when you follow a link.
  • Other websites we link to, such as Apple’s or the FDA’s, are covered by their own policies.

15. Who receives personal data

We don’t sell or rent personal data, and we don’t use it for advertising. Recipients are limited to:

  • Zoho, our email provider (support emails), acting as our processor;
  • GitHub, our website host (technical data from your visit);
  • Apple Inc., which runs TestFlight (the name and email address of beta testers we invite by email; see Beta testing with TestFlight);
  • public authorities, if the law requires us to disclose information.

Apple isn’t our processor. It acts as an independent controller, under its own privacy policy, for the App Store, purchases, TestFlight, crash reports, backups, Apple Health, Apple Intelligence and Age Range for Apps.

If we ever add a service provider that receives personal data, it will have to protect it at least as well as described here, and we’ll update this policy first.

International transfers

Zoho stores our mailbox in the EU. According to Zoho, its staff outside the European Economic Area can access data only in rare technical-support cases, under the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses. GitHub is based in the United States; it complies with the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and also relies on standard contractual clauses. Apple Inc. is also based in the United States. When we invite a beta tester by email, we give Apple the tester’s name and email address because that’s necessary to provide the beta the tester asked to join (GDPR Art. 49(1)(b)). You can ask us for details of these safeguards at any time.

17. Your rights

For personal data we hold about you, such as your emails, the GDPR gives you the right to:

  • access it and get a copy;
  • have it corrected;
  • have it deleted;
  • restrict how we use it;
  • receive it in a portable format;
  • object to processing based on our legitimate interests;
  • withdraw your consent at any time, without affecting what we did before.

We don’t make decisions about you based solely on automated processing.

To use these rights, email admin@ccoders.eu. We’ll answer within one month, or tell you within that month why we need up to two more. We may ask you to confirm it’s you, but we’ll never ask for health details.

What you log in Warmlit never reaches us, so you manage it yourself: you can view, change, export and delete it in the app at any time.

You don’t have to give us any personal data, and Warmlit works without it. If you email us, we need your email address to reply; without it, we can’t answer.

Complaints

You can lodge a complaint with the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (Prezes Urzędu Ochrony Danych Osobowych, UODO), ul. Stanisława Moniuszki 1A, 00-014 Warszawa, Poland, uodo.gov.pl. You can also complain to the data protection authority in the EU country where you live or work, or, in the UK, to the Information Commissioner’s Office (ico.org.uk). We’d welcome the chance to put things right first, so please email us too.

18. Information for US residents

  • We don’t sell personal information, share it for advertising or use it for profiling.
  • We don’t track you, so a Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signal from your browser has nothing left to switch off.
  • Apart from the security logs GitHub keeps when you visit this website (see This website) and the statistics Apple collects if you choose to share analytics with app developers (see Crash reports from Apple), no third party collects personal information about your online activities over time and across websites or apps through Warmlit or this website.
  • We don’t use personal data to train AI models.
  • You can use the rights in Your rights wherever you live. If we decline a request, you can appeal by replying to our answer with the word “Appeal”. We’ll respond in writing within 45 days, and if we deny your appeal, you can complain to your state attorney general.

Consumer health data

If you live in Washington, Nevada or another state with a consumer health data law, please read our separate Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy. In short, the Warmlit app doesn’t collect consumer health data. We receive it only through emails you send us and our beta, we never sell it, and the only party we share it with is Apple, when we invite a beta tester by email.

19. Children

Warmlit is meant for adults who have been prescribed a GLP-1 medicine. It isn’t directed to children, and we don’t knowingly collect personal data from children. If you think a child has sent us personal data, email us and we’ll delete it. Where a US state’s law requires it, Warmlit checks the age range Apple shares and doesn’t open for anyone under 18.

20. Security

  • Without accounts or servers, there’s no central database of your health information to break into.
  • Your entries are protected by the encryption built into iOS. Use a passcode on your iPhone, and turn on Face ID lock in Warmlit for an extra layer.
  • Files you export leave Warmlit’s protection, so store them somewhere safe.
  • Only the people who answer support emails can read them, and our email provider only when it needs to for technical support.
  • This website is served over HTTPS and loads no third-party code.

Before we use personal data we hold about you for a new purpose, we’ll tell you directly, for example by email.
